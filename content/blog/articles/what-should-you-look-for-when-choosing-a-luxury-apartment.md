The difference between an expensive apartment and a luxurious one is not the marble. It is a set of decisions, most of them made long before the finishes were chosen, about where the building stands, which way the homes face and how the whole thing will be looked after. This guide is about those decisions, and how to see them on a visit.

## Orientation and what the windows look at

Start with the compass. In a coastal city, the homes that face the sea are the ones that will always be wanted; the homes that face the road or the next plot are the ones that will always be discounted. In a tower, ask for the floor plan of the *typical floor* and count how many homes on it face the water.

Then think about the sun. A west-facing balcony on this coast gets the evening light and the sunset over the sea; it also gets the afternoon heat. Deep balconies, overhangs and good glass are the answer, and a well-designed building will have them. A home that has been designed for its orientation feels different from one that has been rotated to fit.

## The layout, room by room

Square footage tells you little. Walk the plan as if you lived in it:

- Is the **living space** open to the view, or does it face the corridor?
- Do the **bedrooms** have their own bathrooms, and are they far enough from the living area to be quiet?
- Is there a **real kitchen**, with a utility area that is not the balcony?
- Where do **guests** go, and where do the **staff** work, without crossing each other?
- Is the **balcony** somewhere you would sit, or a ledge for the air-conditioning units?

In larger homes, a 4 BHK or a duplex, ask how the floors relate: whether the private rooms sit above the entertaining rooms, and whether the stair is a feature or an afterthought.

## The building's engineering

This is the part most buyers skip and most regret. A tall building on the coast contends with wind, salt and rain, and the difference between one that has been engineered for those conditions and one that has not shows up in the first monsoon.

Reasonable questions for the developer:

1. Who designed the **structure**, and who advised on **wind**? These are specialist disciplines for a tower.
2. What protects the **reinforcement and façade** from salt air?
3. How is **rainwater** collected and discharged, and how are basements kept dry?
4. How many **lifts** serve the tower, and what is the **power backup** arrangement?

A developer who names their consultants and answers in specifics is telling you the building was designed, not merely drawn.

## Amenities you will use

A long list of amenities is easy to print. The useful test is to imagine an ordinary Tuesday and an ordinary Sunday, and ask which of them you would actually reach for. For most families, the answer is a short list: a pool worth swimming in, a gym, somewhere for children to play, somewhere to walk, somewhere to host. Everything else is pleasant but secondary.

Ask where the amenities are. A clubhouse on a landscaped podium, above the road and open to the sea, is a different experience from one in a basement. Ask, too, what is on the ground: lawns, a forest trail, a place to sit. The ground is the amenity residents use most and brochures mention least.

## The people who will run it

A luxury building is a service as much as a structure. Before buying, find out:

- who will manage the building after handover, and for how long;
- what the **monthly maintenance** will be, and what it covers;
- how the **residents' association** will be formed, and when;
- what the **security** arrangements are, for the tower and for the community around it.

A developer with a long record in the region has a reputation to protect after the last apartment is sold. That is worth more than any single fitting.

> Finishes can be changed. The orientation of a home, the engineering of the building and the ground it stands on cannot.

## The developer's record

Finally, look at what the company has built before, and whether those buildings are still well cared for. Visit one if you can. In Mangalore, the coastal corridor at Kulai offers a useful example: Yamuna Sky City rises within Yamuna Asha City, a gated community that Yamuna Homes and Design has been building for years, so a visitor can see the developer's earlier work from the tower's own site.

## Before you decide

Visit twice, at different times of day. Take the floor plan with you and stand where each room will be. Ask the engineering questions and listen to how they are answered.
