Kulai is a name that appears in more property conversations in Mangalore every year, and often with a question mark after it. Where exactly is it, and why there? This article answers both, with the actual distances rather than adjectives.

## Where Kulai is

Kulai lies on the northern coastal corridor of Mangalore, along National Highway 66, between the city and Surathkal. The area to its south around Panambur and Baikampady is the city's port and industrial belt, home to New Mangalore Port; to its north, the corridor continues towards Surathkal and, further on, Udupi. The Arabian Sea is on the western side of the highway.

This part of the coast is usually called New Mangalore, a loose term for the newer development north of the historic centre. It is where the city has the room to build the kind of residential projects that the dense old centre cannot accommodate.

## What surrounds the site

The immediate neighbourhood around Yamuna Sky City is not a bare highway verge. The tower rises within Yamuna Asha City, a 35-acre gated community developed by Yamuna Homes and Design, which already includes plotted homes, two apartment towers and a restaurant. Two beaches, Kodical and Chithrapura, lie along the shore nearby, with the Honnakatte junction and its temple a short distance inland.

In other words, the tower joins a neighbourhood that exists, rather than promising one that will follow.

## The highway, explained

National Highway 66 is the spine of the west coast, running from Kerala through Mangalore and Udupi to Goa and on to Mumbai. For a resident in Kulai it means three things:

- the city centre and the southern suburbs are a straight drive;
- Udupi, Manipal and the beaches to the north are an easy afternoon;
- the airport at Bajpe is reached without crossing the congested centre.

Living directly on the corridor also means the road is close. A site that steps back from the highway into landscaped ground, with the homes turned towards the sea rather than the road, handles this well. Ask any developer how their site is laid out in relation to the highway; it is a fair question.

## Everyday life within reach

Connectivity is not only about long journeys. A school within four minutes and a large hospital within seven are the distances that shape an ordinary week. The northern corridor has grown its own everyday infrastructure over the past decade, and the fifteen-minute drive to the city centre means the rest of Mangalore, its markets, colleges, restaurants and the old town, is not far when it is wanted.
