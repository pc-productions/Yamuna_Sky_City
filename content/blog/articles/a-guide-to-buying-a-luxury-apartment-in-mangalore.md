Buying a premium apartment is a slower decision than buying an ordinary one, and it should be. The sum is larger, the home is meant to last longer, and the differences between two projects at the same price can be enormous. This guide sets out the questions worth asking, in the order they tend to arise, for anyone buying in Mangalore.

It is written from the point of view of a developer, so we have tried to be honest about which questions buyers should put to us as well.

## 1. Decide what "luxury" means to you before you look

The word covers everything from a large flat with good fittings to a tower with a clubhouse and a private beach path. Before visiting anything, write down the three things that matter most. For most buyers on this coast, the list includes some of these:

- a genuine sea view, not a sliver between two buildings;
- distance from the highway and the airport;
- schools and hospitals within a short drive;
- the size and layout of the home itself: 3 BHK, 4 BHK, a duplex;
- amenities you will actually use, rather than a long list;
- the developer's record in the region.

Knowing your own list stops a good showroom from deciding it for you.

## 2. Choose the stretch of coast

Mangalore's residential geography is simple to describe. The historic centre is dense and largely built. The newer premium corridor runs north along National Highway 66 towards Surathkal, with the sea on the western side of the road. Kulai sits on that corridor.

Each stretch trades one thing for another: the centre for walkable convenience, the northern corridor for space, height and the sea. If you are unfamiliar with the northern side, our piece on [Kulai and New Mangalore's connectivity](/blog/kulai-and-new-mangalore-understanding-the-areas-growing-connectivity) is a useful starting point.

## 3. Check the RERA registration first

Every residential project of any size in Karnataka must be registered with the Karnataka Real Estate Regulatory Authority (K-RERA) before it can be marketed or sold. The registration number should appear on the developer's website, brochure and advertisements.

Look the number up on the K-RERA portal yourself. The record shows the promoter, the approved plans, the declared completion timeline and the quarterly progress the developer is obliged to file. If a project is being sold without a registration number, or the number cannot be found, stop there.

Yamuna Sky City's registration number is printed in the footer of every page of this website, which is where it should be.

## 4. Understand what area you are paying for

Indian apartments are quoted in several different areas, and the difference between them is not small:

| Term | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Carpet area | The usable floor area inside the walls of the home (the RERA definition also includes internal walls, but not balconies or common areas) |
| Built-up area | Carpet area plus the thickness of external walls and balconies |
| Super built-up area | Built-up area plus a share of common spaces: lobbies, corridors, the clubhouse |

RERA requires developers to state the carpet area. Ask for it, and compare projects on carpet area rather than on the largest number in the brochure. A brochure that quotes a "saleable area" is quoting super built-up; ask what percentage of it is common area.

## 5. Title, approvals and the paper trail

The unglamorous part of a purchase is the one that protects you. For an apartment under construction, the documents to ask for are:

1. **Title documents** for the land, and an **encumbrance certificate** showing no outstanding claims on it.
2. **The sanctioned building plan** from the local planning authority, matching what is being built.
3. **The RERA registration** and the agreement for sale in the RERA-prescribed form.
4. **Completion and occupancy certificates**, once the building is finished; do not take possession without them.

A lawyer who does property work in Dakshina Kannada will review all of this for a modest fee. It is money well spent, whoever the developer is.

## 6. Ask about the building, not just the apartment

On the coast, the building matters more than it does inland. Salt air, wind loads on a tall structure and heavy monsoon rain all place demands on design and construction. Sensible questions:

- Who are the structural and façade consultants, and have they worked on tall coastal buildings before?
- How is rainwater handled on the site, and how are the basements protected?
- What is the maintenance arrangement after handover, and what will it cost per month?
- How many lifts serve how many homes, and what happens during a power cut?

A developer who welcomes these questions is telling you something. So is one who does not.

## 7. Look at the ground, not just the tower

A premium home is also the land around it. Ask how much of the plot is landscaped, what sits on the podium, and whether the amenities are designed for the residents who will live there or for the photographs in the brochure. A 45,000 square foot clubhouse on a podium, an infinity pool facing the sea, a forest trail: these are the kinds of specifics a developer should be willing to show on a plan, not describe in adjectives.

## 8. Visit at two different times of day

Morning light and evening light show different things. So does a weekday versus a Sunday, when you can hear the neighbourhood. If the sea is the reason you are buying, stand where your balcony will be, or as close as the site allows, and look.
